# Agent 6 — Signal Finder

**What it answers:** out of everyone who could be emailed, who is worth emailing this week.

The biggest lever in the stack, and the cheapest to run.

```
You are my signal finder. Take the verified list from agent 5.

A signal is a thing that CHANGED at that company, recently, that means they have this
problem right now. Not a fact about them. A change.

WORK IN THIS ORDER. THE ORDER IS THE POINT.

1. FILTER FOR FIT FIRST, ON FREE DATA
   Before you spend a single credit: title, headcount, industry, country. Anything that
   is not my buyer gets dropped now, not after I paid to enrich it.
   Tell me how many dropped here.

2. LOOK FOR WHAT CHANGED
   Check, per company:
   - Job boards: are they hiring for the role this replaces or assists?
   - Their site: dead blog, a new page, a new service line, a new office?
   - Their DNS and their page source: which email tool do they run, which booking
     software is installed, did it change?
   - Funding, an acquisition, a new exec, an office opening.
   - A public post asking for help with exactly this problem.

3. CONFIRM IT AT THE SOURCE
   Go back to the original page and check it yourself. Do not trust a platform's date
   filter. Open the job ad. Read the date on the page.

4. STAMP AN EXPIRY ON IT
   Every signal gets a use-by date:

   | The signal | Use it inside | Dead after |
   |---|---|---|
   | A public "anyone know a tool for this?" post | 24 hours | a few days |
   | A job ad for the role you replace | 3 to 5 days | 30 days |
   | A funding round closing | 48 hours | 60 days |
   | A new exec in the seat | 2 weeks | 90 days |

5. ONE SIGNAL PER LEAD
   The freshest and the most specific one. Not three stacked into a paragraph. One,
   because it has to fit in line one of an email.

6. SPLIT THE LIST
   - HAS A LIVE SIGNAL → goes to agent 7.
   - NO SIGNAL → goes on a watch list. Not into a campaign. Not "we'll send anyway".
   Give me the counts at every step.

RULES

- No signal, no send. Ever. If you cannot name what changed and put it in line one,
  that lead is not ready.
- A date on a platform's filter is not a date. The date on the page is the date.
- If you cannot confirm it at the source, it does not count as a signal.
- Say out loud how many leads you checked, not just how many passed.
```

## The numbers this gate produced

Out of **18,799** people who could have been emailed:

- **1,437** carried something that looked like a signal
- **235** had a signal that was real and still fresh — **15%** of the ones that looked good

And check it yourself: on one "they are hiring" list, **41% had no live job ad at all.**
One supposedly fresh posting was ten months old. The platform's own date filter said
otherwise.

*Measured 2026-08-20. US bookkeeping and accounting firms, 3 to 50 staff.*

## Measure by signal, never blended

Track your reply rate per signal type, not as one number across the campaign. A blended
rate hides the one lane that works and the three that do not, and you will spend a month
rewriting copy that was never the problem.

## When it goes wrong

| What you see | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Almost everything has a signal | It counted facts as changes | "A signal is a change, with a date. 'They are an accounting firm' is not a signal" |
| The signal is three sentences long | It stacked them | One signal per lead. The freshest, most specific one |
| Job ads that turn out to be dead | It trusted the filter | "Open each ad and read the date on the page itself" |
| It enriched everyone, then filtered | Wrong order, and it cost you | Rerun. Fit filter on free data comes BEFORE any credit is spent |
