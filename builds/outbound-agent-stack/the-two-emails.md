# The two emails

The whole campaign. Forty words, then one follow-up, then nothing.

Copy the shape, not the words. The words came from agent 2 reading what small law firms
say to each other. Yours will be different, and they should be.

---

## Subject line

```
who reconciles the trust account
```

Five words. Lower case. Not a question we wrote — it is what they call the thing they
are afraid of.

---

## Email 1 — 40 words

```
Hi {first name},

Saw {their firm} handles {their practice area} work out of {their city}.

We run the books and the monthly trust reconciliation for small law firms. Nobody there
has to sit and check the ledger.

Can I send some more info?
```

**What each line is doing**

| Line | Job | Pinned to |
|---|---|---|
| Line 1 | Proves this was not blasted | Their firm name as written on their own site |
| Line 1 | The signal, in one clause | Their practice area, in their words, from their practice page |
| Line 2 | The end result that signal implies | Never a pain line. Never the offer. What they get |
| Line 3 | The smallest possible ask | "Can I send some more info?" — verbatim |

**What is not in it**

No price. No case study. No client numbers. No link. No calendar. No "quick question".
No "hope this finds you well".

Email 1 earns a reply. It does not close.

---

## Email 2 — day two, same thread, no new subject

```
Hey {first name},

Just got some time to get back to you.

Pulled together a month-end trust account checklist — the red flags worth catching
yourself before an auditor does.

Want me to send it over?
```

**This is the one that worked.**

It asks for nothing. It offers something they would keep even if they never reply. And
the thing it offers is real — it exists, it took an afternoon to make, and it gets sent
the moment they say yes.

If you do not have the thing, make the thing. An offer of a document that does not exist
is the fastest way to lose the reply you just earned.

---

## Then it stops

Two emails. There is no third.

No "just bumping this to the top of your inbox". No "did you get a chance to look".
No "out of respect for your time, I'll stop here" — that one is a guilt trip wearing a
manners costume, and buyers read it that way.

Two, then stop, then that lead goes back on the watch list for the next signal.

---

## What came back, that evening

> "wha'ts your number"
>
> — a law firm in Maryland, replying to the second email

Inside two weeks that firm had signed with our client, a bookkeeping firm in San Diego,
at **$2,500 a month**.

The typo is in the original. That is what a real reply looks like: fast, sloppy, and
asking to be sold to.

---

## How to load it

Each signal is its **own campaign**, with its own list.

Never load signal-routed copy as variants of one campaign. Sending tools rotate variants
at random across the whole list. Your hiring-signal email goes to the funding-signal
leads, every enrichment credit you spent is wasted, and nothing in the dashboard tells
you it happened.

One signal, one campaign, one list, and the routing rule written next to it.

Variants are only ever a true A/B: same list, same signal, two phrasings, both tagged.
