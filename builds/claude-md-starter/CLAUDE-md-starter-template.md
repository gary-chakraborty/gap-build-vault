# The CLAUDE.md Starter Template

## What this file actually is

CLAUDE.md is just a plain text file. You save it in the folder where you work with
Claude, and Claude reads it at the start of every session, before it reads anything else
you type. Once it is set up, you stop explaining your business from scratch every single
chat, because Claude already knows it.

This document has two parts:

1. The **blank starter template** below, section by section, with notes on what goes in
   each slot.
2. A **fully filled example** for a made up company, so you can see exactly what a
   finished file looks like. The same example also lives on its own in
   `CLAUDE-md-example-filled.md`, so you can open it side by side while you fill in yours.

Copy the blank template into a new file, fill in every bracket with your own facts, delete
the brackets, save it, and you are done. This is not a teaser version. Every section here
is the same shape we use to run a real business on Claude.

## PART 1 - THE BLANK STARTER TEMPLATE

Copy everything between the lines below into a new text file. Replace every `[BRACKET]`
with your own real answer. If a section does not apply to you yet, leave a single honest
line like "not decided yet" rather than deleting the heading. Claude reading "not decided
yet" behaves better than Claude reading nothing.

```

never break. Read this at the start of every session.

[WHAT YOU ACTUALLY DELIVER, described the way you would say it out loud to a stranger,
not the way it reads on your website].

What we sell: [THE OFFER, one or two sentences, plain words].

Price: [YOUR REAL CURRENT PRICE OR PRICE RANGE]. This is the only price. If a past
document says something different, this file wins.

Guarantee or promise, if you make one: [STATE IT EXACTLY, with the real number and the
real condition. A vague promise is worse than none, because Claude will repeat it
exactly as vague as you wrote it].

Rules for how we talk about price: [FOR EXAMPLE: never round a number down to sound
cheaper, never quote a price without also saying what it includes, never discount in
writing without asking me first].

## MY ICP (WHO I ACTUALLY SELL TO)

Company shape: [STAFF COUNT RANGE, REVENUE RANGE, INDUSTRY OR INDUSTRIES].

Title I sell to: [THE JOB TITLE OR TITLES OF THE PERSON WHO SAYS YES].

What makes someone a good fit: [THE 2-4 REAL TRAITS THAT MATTER, not a vertical label.
```

## PART 2 - HOW TO KEEP THIS FILE SMALL

A CLAUDE.md file gets read on every single chat, so a bloated file slows everything down
and buries the rules that matter under the ones that do not. Three habits keep it lean:

1. **Set a size limit and write it at the top of the file.** A simple line like "this file
   stays under 2000 words, if you add a section here, cut one somewhere else" forces
   real editing instead of endless adding.
2. **Keep only always-needed facts in this file.** Anything you only need occasionally
   (a long list of past objections, a detailed style guide, a full case study library)
   goes in a separate file, and this file just points to it by name. Claude only opens
   that file when the task actually needs it.
3. **Review it every month.** Delete anything that stopped being true. A rule about a
   price you no longer charge is worse than no rule at all, because Claude will follow it
   exactly.

## PART 3 - THE FILLED EXAMPLE

Below is the same template, completely filled in, for a made up company called Marlow
Creative Co. Every name, number, and detail here is invented for this example. The exact
same file also lives on its own in `CLAUDE-md-example-filled.md` if you want a clean copy
to read side by side while you build yours.

```
# CLAUDE.md - Dana Marlow / Marlow Creative Co.

This file tells Claude who I am, what my business does, how I work, and the rules I
never break. Read this at the start of every session.

## WHO I AM AND WHAT MY BUSINESS DOES

I am Dana Marlow, I run Marlow Creative Co. We are a demand generation agency for
mid market B2B software companies. We run paid LinkedIn and Meta campaigns and rebuild
landing pages so more of the traffic our clients already pay for actually converts.

We work with software companies between 5 million and 50 million dollars in yearly
revenue, usually with a marketing team of 4 to 15 people. Our main service is a monthly
retainer covering ad management, landing page testing, and a weekly numbers review call.

## MY OFFER AND PRICING RULES

What we sell: a done for you paid demand generation program. We build the campaigns, we
build and test the landing pages, we report the real numbers every week.

Price: three tiers, three month minimum. Starter is 6,240 dollars a month. Growth is
11,485 dollars a month. Scale is 19,730 dollars a month. These are the only three prices.
If an old proposal says something different, this file wins.

Guarantee: if we do not get a client 22 marketing qualified leads within the first 63
days, we keep running the campaign until they hit 22, and they are not billed for those
extra months.

Rules for how we talk about price: never round a tier price down to sound cheaper, never
quote a price without saying which tier it is, never discount in writing without asking
Dana first.

## MY ICP (WHO I ACTUALLY SELL TO)

Company shape: B2B software companies, 5 to 50 million dollars in yearly revenue, a
marketing team of 4 to 15 people.

Title I sell to: VP of Marketing or Head of Growth, the person who owns the ad budget.

What makes someone a good fit: already running paid ads somewhere, so budget is proven,
not hoped for. Using a real CRM, not a spreadsheet. Has a repeatable sales process the
new leads can actually flow into. Has been burned by an agency that reported clicks
instead of pipeline.

What makes someone a bad fit, even if they ask: pre revenue startups with no sales
process yet, and companies wanting a one month test with no minimum. Both waste our time
and theirs.

## WRITING RULES

Write everything at a level a 12 year old could read out loud without stumbling. Short
sentences. Plain words. Active voice, not passive.

No corporate language. Say "we call your leads back the same day," never "we optimize
response time within a defined SLA window."

Always use specific numbers, never round ones, in anything that claims a result. "Grew
from 14 leads a month to 37" is believable. "Grew from 10 to 40" reads like a guess,
because it is one.

Banned words: "synergy," "leverage" used as a verb, "revolutionary," "game changing."
A client called our first draft deck "salesy" in June, so also banned: "unlock,"
"supercharge."

## OUTPUT STANDARDS

- Subject lines under 5 words.
- No bullet points inside an email body.
- Every call to action offers two real times, never a scheduling link.
- Every finished piece of writing is ready to send as is, no placeholder text left in it.
- Every case study number is the real, specific number, never rounded.

## THINGS CLAUDE SHOULD NEVER DO

- Never quote a price that is not one of the three tiers above.
- Never promise a lead number that is not 22 in 63 days, that is the only guarantee.
- Never send anything client-facing without a real number in it, not a placeholder.
- Never make Dana a check-in step for something a written process already covers. Do the
  task, and only ask Dana when nothing written covers the situation.

## HOW I WORK WITH CLAUDE

When I say "draft an email" I mean a full ready-to-send email, not an outline. When I
upload a document, read the whole thing before answering. When I ask for a client update,
check the real numbers first, never guess a metric.
```
