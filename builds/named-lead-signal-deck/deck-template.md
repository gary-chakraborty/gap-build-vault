# The signal deck: structure and slot rules

A signal deck is 6 to 8 real companies in your ICP, each with a live buying signal and a
written opener. Four columns, one row per company. This page is the exact structure plus the
rules for what goes in each column, then one full worked example so you can see it done.

## The four columns

| Column | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Company | The real, named company plus a one-line size and industry tag |
| Live signal | One dated, public event, plus where you found it |
| Why it matters | One sentence tying that exact signal to what changes for that company |
| The opener | Under 64 words, written as if you were reaching out to them today |

## Slot rules, read these before you fill in a single row

### Company

State the real company name plus a one-line descriptor: industry and a rough size band.
Anyone reading the deck should be able to search the name and find the company in ten
seconds.

- Wrong: "A growing logistics company in the Midwest."
- Right: "Harrow Point Logistics, regional 3PL, around 184 staff, Reno NV."

### Live signal

One event. It has to pass three tests, all three, no exceptions:

1. **Public**, you can point to where you found it. A news piece, a press release, a job
   posting, a company's own site.
2. **Dated**, you know when it happened, and it's recent. Use a 90-day window as your
   default. Give leadership changes a 12-month window, since those take longer to change
   buying behavior.
3. **Causal**, you can say in one plain sentence why this event makes the company more
   likely to need what you sell, right now, more than it did six months ago.

- Wrong: "They're growing fast." No date, nothing to check, no reason given.
- Right: "Opened a new 138,500-square-foot distribution center in Reno, covered in the local
  business journal, March 2026."

**The trap:** if the same signal is true of every company on your list, it isn't a signal, it's
a market condition, and the deck will read like a filtered spreadsheet. Mix signal types across
your 6 to 8 rows: a new hire here, a hiring spike there, a funding round somewhere else. See
`signal-research-prompt.md` for the five signal types to hunt for.

**No source, no row.** If you can't find and open the page that proves the signal in about
three minutes, drop that company. Do not soften the wording and keep it in. A deck of six
verified companies beats one of eight with two guesses.

### Why it matters

One sentence. It has to be specific to THIS company's signal, not a line you could paste under
every row unchanged.

- Wrong: "Growing companies always need more help." (True of everyone, proves nothing.)
- Right: "A facility that size usually outpaces what their current hiring channels can fill
  inside a normal ramp-up window."

Quick check: read your six "why it matters" lines back to back. If two of them are
interchangeable, you've written a pain point, not a reason. Rewrite the weaker one.

### The opener

Under 64 words. Count them before you call it done. Two lines, in this order:

1. **The signal, stated back to them.** Not a compliment, not small talk, the actual dated
   event.
2. **The end result that signal implies.** What changes for them because of it. Never your
   pitch, never your price, never a meeting ask.

No bullet points. No calendar link. Write it at a level a 12-year-old could read out loud
without stumbling. If you wrote your company name, your offer, or a price anywhere in the
opener, delete it and start over, that's a pitch, not a proof point.

- Wrong: "Hi, I saw you're expanding, we help companies like yours hire faster. Want to grab
  15 minutes this week?" (Pitches, asks for the meeting, could apply to any company.)
- Right: see row 1 in the worked example below.

## The worked example: six fictional rows, one fictional reader

Every name below is invented. None of it is a real company, a real signal, or a real client of
any business. This is what a finished deck looks like once you've done the work.

**The scenario:** imagine you run a staffing agency that places warehouse and supply-chain
operations talent at mid-size third-party logistics (3PL) companies. This is the deck you'd
build for that ICP.

---

**1. Harrow Point Logistics**, regional 3PL, around 184 staff, Reno NV

- **Live signal:** Opened a new 138,500-square-foot distribution center in Reno, covered in
  the local business journal, March 2026.
- **Why it matters:** A facility that size usually outpaces what their current hiring channels
  can fill inside a normal ramp-up window.
- **The opener:** "Saw Harrow Point's new Reno facility went live last month. A site that size
  usually means the ops team is filling warehouse lead and shift supervisor roles faster than
  the usual channels can keep up with."

---

**2. Cascade Freight Partners**, mid-size freight carrier, around 236 staff, Columbus OH

- **Live signal:** Brought on a new VP of Operations, announced on the company's site,
  January 2026.
- **Why it matters:** A new VP in that seat usually means a fresh look at who's filling floor
  and dock roles for the next two quarters.
- **The opener:** "Noticed Cascade Freight brought on a new VP of Operations in January. A new
  VP in that seat usually means a fresh look at who's staffing the floor for the next two
  quarters."

---

**3. Briarwood Supply Chain Co.**, regional distributor, around 267 staff, three states

- **Live signal:** 14 open warehouse associate roles posted across three states in the past
  six weeks, found on their careers page and major job boards.
- **Why it matters:** That volume of open roles usually means the internal team is stretched
  past what job boards alone can fill in time.
- **The opener:** "Counted 14 open warehouse roles Briarwood has posted across three states in
  the past six weeks. That kind of volume usually means the internal team needs more than job
  boards alone can deliver in time."

---

**4. Union Bay Distribution**, growth-stage distributor, around 163 staff

- **Live signal:** Closed a $22.4 million growth funding round, announced by the investing
  firm, April 2026.
- **Why it matters:** Rounds that size usually come with a hiring plan bigger than the current
  team was built to run alone.
- **The opener:** "Saw Union Bay closed a $22.4 million growth round in April. Rounds that
  size usually come with a hiring plan the current team wasn't built to run by itself."

---

**5. Tallgrass Intermodal**, intermodal freight operator, around 198 staff

- **Live signal:** Added refrigerated freight as a new service line, announced on their
  newsroom page, May 2026.
- **Why it matters:** A specialized new line like that usually means new roles nobody on the
  current team has hired for before.
- **The opener:** "Noticed Tallgrass added refrigerated freight as a new line in May. A line
  that specialized usually means roles nobody on the current team has had to hire for
  before."

---

**6. Redline Carrier Solutions**, regional carrier, around 172 staff

- **Live signal:** Facing a new federal electronic-logging compliance deadline in June 2026,
  listed on the public federal register.
- **Why it matters:** Deadlines like that usually pull ops leads off hiring for months, which
  is exactly when open roles sit the longest.
- **The opener:** "Saw Redline is facing the new electronic-logging deadline in June.
  Deadlines like that usually pull ops leads off hiring for months, right when open roles sit
  the longest."

---

That's the shape. Six rows, six different signal types, six openers that name the event and
stop. Nobody gets pitched, nobody gets asked for a meeting, and every line can be checked by
the person reading it.
