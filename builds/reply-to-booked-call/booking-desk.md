# The booking desk

This is everything that happens between "sounds good" and the call actually taking place. It is not exciting, and it
is where most booked calls quietly die. Nobody sent the reminder. Nobody had a plan for the no-show. Nobody rang the
person who went quiet.

## One person owns it

One person owns every booked call, from the moment it is booked until it happens. Not "the team". A name.

When two people share it, both of them assume the other one sent the reminder, and neither of them did.

## The call list has two columns

Whoever is making the calls works from one list per situation: today's calls, the no-shows, the people who went quiet.
Each row has their name, their number, and exactly two columns of context.

**"What happened."** Two to four plain sentences. How they found you, what they said, where things stand. The last
sentence is the line you open the call with.

**"Their last message."** Their own words, copied out, plus one line saying what they were replying to.

That is it. Do not split the story into five columns for what they said, what you said, how they sounded and what tags
apply. Somebody on a live call cannot put five boxes back together in their head. And if something is missing, write
that it is missing. Never leave a box blank, because a blank box looks like a zero.

## What you send, and when

| When | What goes out | Where |
|---|---|---|
| As soon as they book | A short confirmation, in the same thread they replied in | Wherever they came from |
| The day before | One line: the time, plus one question about their business for the call | Same thread |
| An hour before | Only if they have not confirmed | Text or LinkedIn |
| They did not show, same day | "Missed you today, happens to everyone. Want to pick a new time?" Offer two times | Same thread |
| They did not show, two days later | One more try, then leave it | Phone |

Run these as a follow-up sequence that the person gets moved into, not as a new cold sequence. They already know you.
A cold opener to somebody who booked a call with you reads like you forgot them.

## Count the right things

Three rules, and every one of them changes the number you see.

**A call counts on the day it is booked**, not the day it happens. Calls booked this week for next Tuesday are this
week's work. Count them any other way and you will cut the thing that is working.

**A rebook is not a new person, and a reschedule is not a new call.** Count people, once each.

**Divide by the number of people you contacted**, never by the number of messages you sent. One person gets two
emails, so dividing by messages roughly halves your own reply rate.
