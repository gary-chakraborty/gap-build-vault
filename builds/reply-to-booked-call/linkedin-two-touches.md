# LinkedIn, in two messages

This is the LinkedIn side of the system. Same rule as email: a stranger hears from you twice, then you stop.

## First, work out which way in you have

LinkedIn gives you two ways to reach somebody, and they do not reach the same people. Pick the wrong one and your
message is never delivered. You are not told. It just does not arrive.

**The connection request.** You send a request with a short note on it. When they accept, you can message them. This
works on anybody.

**The InMail.** You message somebody without connecting first. This only works if that person has turned on a setting
called Open Profile. Most people have not. If you send it to everybody, LinkedIn quietly cancels the ones it cannot
deliver, and you never find out who they were.

So check who has Open Profile turned on. Those people can get an InMail. Everybody else gets a connection request.

Build these as **two separate sequences with two separate lists**. If you mix them into one, your LinkedIn tool will
throw away every message it cannot send, and your numbers will look like nobody was interested.

## The two messages

| | Connection request lane | InMail lane |
|---|---|---|
| First message | The request, with a note about the thing that just changed at their company. Under 300 characters, because that is all LinkedIn allows | One message. The first line is the thing that just changed. Under 64 words |
| Second message | After they accept: one message, with one question they can answer with a fact | One follow-up, three or four days later. Same thing: one question |
| After that | Stop | Stop |

## Why the question matters

Ask "is this something you are thinking about?" and you get "yes", or nothing. Neither tells you anything.

Ask "how many new clients came in last month without a referral?" and you get a number. A number is a conversation,
because now you have something real to talk about.

## Three things that break this

**The sequence locks when it starts.** In most LinkedIn tools you can change the words in a running sequence, but you
cannot add another message to it. So decide you are sending two, and only two, before you press start.

**"Thanks for connecting" is not a message.** It burns your second touch on nothing. Your second message is the
question.

**Somebody who is already talking to you is not a stranger.** If they viewed your profile, commented on your post, or
messaged you first, they get the [warm reply ladder](warm-reply-ladder.md) instead. Sending a cold opener to a warm
person reads like you forgot who they are.
