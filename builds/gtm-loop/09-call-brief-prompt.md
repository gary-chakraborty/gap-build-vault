# 09 · The call brief (before)

Run it the morning of the call. One page. You read it once before the call, not during.

```text
I have a sales call today. Build me a one-page brief. My business file is in project knowledge;
read it first and use only the offer, price and proof in it. Never use an older number.

The person: [NAME], [TITLE] at [COMPANY], [WEBSITE]
What we sent them: [PASTE THE MESSAGE THEY REPLIED TO]
What they said: [PASTE THEIR REPLY, EXACTLY]
Call length: [MINUTES]

Research their company for 10 minutes: what they sell, who to, roughly how big, anything that
changed in the last 90 days (hiring, funding, new leader, new location, a public post about a
problem). Every fact gets a date and a link. No date, leave it out.

Then write, in this order:

1. THE 3 THINGS THAT WILL LOSE THIS CALL. Specific to this person, not general advice.
2. WHO THEY ARE. Five lines, facts only.
3. THE OPEN. One line that confirms what they do (never ask them to explain their own
   business), then ask permission for the agenda.
4. QUALIFY. Three questions that tell me in 4 minutes whether they can buy. A clean, polite
   exit line if they cannot.
5. DISCOVERY. Five questions that go deep on one pain: what it costs, what they tried, why now.
   Questions only. No pitching in this section.
6. THE REASON WE WROTE. The one dated change that put them on our list, in one line, and the
   message we sent, word for word.
7. OFFER AND PRICE. Their words back to them, then the offer and the price in two sentences,
   then "stop talking".
8. LIKELY PUSHBACK. Up to 4, as short dialogue. For each: first find out if it is the only
   thing in the way, then answer it.
9. THE CLOSE. One question that asks for the decision.

Rules: conversation, not a script. Under 600 words. No claim about my results that is not in
my business file.
```

**You know this worked when:** you walk in knowing the one change at their company and the one
pain you will spend the most time on.
