# 10 · The call score (after)

Record the call with permission. Transcribe it (Whisper, or your call tool's transcript).
Paste the transcript below. Run it the same day.

**Do this once first:** ask your best model to turn your sales training notes into a written
rubric (the nine checks below are a starting point), and save it to project knowledge. Then
any cheaper model can score every call against it. The rubric carries the judgment.

```text
Score this sales call. First tell me what kind of call it is: a booking call (the goal is a
next meeting) or a closing call (the goal is a decision). Use the matching rubric. Do not score
a booking call on closing skills.

For a closing call, score each check PASS / FAIL / NOT REACHED, with the timestamp:

1. Frame. Who asked most of the questions in the first 10 minutes?
2. Depth. Did we find the cost of the problem and why it matters now, in their numbers?
3. Their words. Did they say the problem out loud themselves, or did we tell them?
4. Offer stated on the call, not "I'll send something over".
5. Price said plainly, once, then silence.
6. Pushback narrowed to one thing before answering it.
7. The first stall ("let me think about it") met once, calmly, with a next step and a date.
8. A direct ask for the decision, then silence.
9. Conviction. Did the delivery sound sure, or did it trail off?

Then:
- WHERE IT SLIPPED: the single moment the call turned, with the timestamp and their exact words.
- THE LINE: what to say instead at that moment, word for word.
- ONE DRILL for the next call.

Talk time: estimate my share of the talking. Over 60% on a discovery call is a problem.

TRANSCRIPT:
[PASTE]
```

**You know this worked when:** the score points at one moment and gives you the exact line,
not a general tip.
