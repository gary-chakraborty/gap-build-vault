The work in four steps

We found every name in public records. We checked each one by hand before we wrote to anybody.

1

Set the rules

We wrote down your filters from our calls: the owner only, no government work, nobody who wants a bookkeeper in their office, under 100 staff, firms the size that pays $750 to $2,000 a month.

2

Watch for the moment

We tracked four public records that say a company just got busier. Each one has a date on it, so we know how fresh it is.

3

Find the owner, check the address

We found the owner by name and title, then verified every email address before use. No guessed addresses, no bought lists.

4

Write to them as a founder

Two short emails each, naming the specific thing we noticed. Nothing went out until you approved the wording.

Two things also hold us back. We had open tracking switched off, so we cannot see who read the emails. And we have not written to 20 of the owners we found.

What we had, and what we did not

On a free round we use whatever is already public. When you hire us properly, we start from inside your business, and that is where the sharper targeting comes from.

What we worked from

  • Your rules. You gave us these on our calls.
  • Public records. We read permits, state filings and job ads.
  • Your approval. You signed off every email before we sent it.
  • What ProLedge does. You described it to us in your own words.

What we never had

  • A sample monthly report. Our second email offers one. You never sent us a page, so we had nothing to offer them. When we offer our other clients' prospects something useful, they reply.
  • Your current client list. We wanted to be certain we never wrote to somebody you already look after.
  • Which clients you like most. You told us they run from $6,000 to $25,000 a year. We did not know which end to hunt for.
  • How many new clients you can take. You never gave us a number.

What we are aiming at

You told us one client is worth $6,000 to $25,000 a year to ProLedge. So you cover most of a year of our fee with one new client.

  • We write to enough owners each month that the reply rate means something. Not 46 in total.
  • We keep whichever of the four reasons brings replies, and we drop the ones that do not.
  • We send your sample report page in the second email, so it carries something worth opening.
  • You talk to owners who look like the clients you already like.

We find names by hand, so we only add about five owners a week. Until a machine does the finding, you will never get a list big enough to judge.
